Archimedean copulas are defined through a generator function ($\phi$):
$$
C(u,v)=\phi^{-1}(\phi(u)+\phi(v))
$$
They are popular because they are easy to calibrate and interpret.

Clayton copula:
- Stronger lower-tail dependence.
- Useful when joint downside events matter more than joint upside events.

Gumbel copula:
- Stronger upper-tail dependence.
- Useful when extreme positive co-movements matter.

Compare quickly:
- Clayton -> downside clustering.
- Gumbel -> upside clustering.
- Choose based on empirical tail behavior, not convenience.
